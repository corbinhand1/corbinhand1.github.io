JumpAloft Privacy Policy

Last updated: 28 November 2026

JumpAloft is an iOS weather and planning app for skydivers. It has no accounts, no advertising and no tracking. This policy describes what the app sends, to whom, and why. It matches the disclosure in JumpAloft Settings (About) and the app’s privacy manifest.

What leaves your device

  • The drop zone you select. Its directory id is sent to the JumpAloft API (api.jumpaloft.com) to load winds aloft, the Outlook and the wind map for it.
  • Coordinates of a place you add. For a custom place, its coordinates are sent to the JumpAloft API, which rounds them to a cell of about 2 km and samples forecasts there. The cell is shared by everyone who saves a place in it and is not linked to you. It stops being sampled after 180 days without use. The coordinates may also be used to look up the ground elevation (JumpAloft API) and the time zone (Apple).
  • Nearby airport reports. The selected place’s coordinates are sent to the NOAA Aviation Weather Center (aviationweather.gov) to find nearby airport observations (METAR) and pilot reports.
  • The map area you view. The wind map loads its base map (OpenFreeMap), terrain shading (public elevation tiles) and radar and satellite images through the JumpAloft API, so those services do not see your device. Other maps in the app are Apple’s (MapKit).
  • Loads and planes. Loads at a drop zone and the planes near it come from the JumpAloft API, which reads the drop zone’s public load board and public aircraft data. The app never receives the names on a board. If you choose to follow your name on a board, that name (or the letters you search with) is sent to the JumpAloft API to find your loads. It is kept there only as a scrambled code tied to this copy of the app, never as the name.
  • A signature request. If you ask someone to sign logbook jumps by link or QR code, your first name and those jumps’ facts (date, drop zone, altitude, freefall, type, equipment) are held on the JumpAloft API for up to 24 hours under a random code. The signer’s name, number and signature are held there only until your iPhone collects them. Then it is all deleted. The rest of your profile (license, ratings and so on) is never sent to JumpAloft.
  • An install code. Requests to the JumpAloft API carry a random install code made by the app (not taken from your device), the app version, and whether the device is an iPhone or iPad, so installs can be counted. The server keeps it with a location rounded to about 50 km, and never your IP address. Deleting the app deletes the code. It is not tied to an account and not used to track you.

Your device location, and the barometer

JumpAloft asks for location only when you use a feature that needs it, and your device location is not sent to the JumpAloft API or any other service.

  • Use My Location measures straight-line distance to drop zones, on your iPhone.
  • Cutaway Finder: during a search you start, your location points you to your canopy and records where you have searched, also with the phone locked, until you pause or end the search or tap Found It. A search that nobody moves or tends stops by itself after half an hour or four hours. The search track and your finds stay on your iPhone. They leave it only if you share the search map yourself.
  • Detect My Jumps (optional, off until you turn it on): at one of your saved drop zones, your iPhone’s barometer and location are read to notice a climb and a fall and offer the jump for your logbook, also with the phone locked. If you allow “Always” location and tap “Start on Its Own When I Arrive”, iOS tells JumpAloft when you arrive at a saved drop zone, so it can start watching without being opened. Nothing is recorded anywhere else. The readings stay on your iPhone and are not sent to JumpAloft. A jump you log, with its recorded flight, is kept in your logbook (see “Your iCloud” below).

What the JumpAloft API records

The JumpAloft API runs on Cloudflare. For a sample of requests it records usage statistics: the kind of request, the drop zone id, the forecast model, how long it took and whether it succeeded, and an approximate location derived by Cloudflare from the network address (country, region, city, and a position rounded to about 50 km). Your IP address is not stored, and nothing is linked to you or your device. These statistics are used to keep the service running and to see which drop zones and features are used.

Your iCloud

If you are signed in to iCloud, your logbook (its jumps, recorded flights and signatures), your jumper profile (name, birthday, license, ratings), your coaches and recent signers, your count of earlier jumps, and your reserve repack and AAD dates are kept in your own iCloud, so they open on your other iPhones, iPads and Macs signed in to the same Apple ID. They are stored in your private iCloud storage with Apple. JumpAloft the developer cannot read, collect or analyze them, and has no server that holds them. If you are not signed in to iCloud, or iCloud is off or full, they stay on the device. Deleting a jump deletes it on all your devices. Apple’s iCloud terms and privacy policy apply to that storage.

On your device only

Saved places, favorites, settings, Spot setups, cached weather, Cutaway Finder searches and finds, the load you mark as yours, your call alerts, the readings Detect My Jumps takes, and any FlySight file you import stay on the device they were made on. A FlySight file is read on the device and is not uploaded. Alerts, reminders, Lock Screen cards and widgets are made on your device. JumpAloft has no account backend.

What JumpAloft does not do

  • No accounts, login, or Sign in with Apple.
  • No advertising, no analytics or crash-reporting SDK in the app.
  • No tracking as defined by Apple (NSPrivacyTracking is false), and no selling or sharing of data.

Data sources and credits

Forecasts come from NOAA (RRFS, HRRR, GFS, MRMS radar, GOES satellite) and ECMWF open data (CC BY 4.0), served by the JumpAloft API. Map data: © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL), OpenMapTiles, OpenFreeMap. Each provider processes requests under its own terms.

Children

JumpAloft is a weather and skydiving planning tool for adults. It does not knowingly collect personal information from children; it has no accounts.
